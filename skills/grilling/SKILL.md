---
name: grilling
description: Stress-test a plan, decision, or idea through evidence and consequential tradeoffs. Use when the user asks to grill or challenge their thinking.
---

Investigate the relevant implementation, constraints, and recorded decisions before
asking questions. Resolve discoverable facts yourself. Reuse settled decisions;
reopen one only when new evidence creates a material conflict, explaining why.

Ask about unresolved choices with materially different product outcomes, risks,
or tradeoffs. Give a recommendation, its reason, and a concrete example. Challenge
weak assumptions candidly, including the user's preferred answer when warranted.

Choose routine engineering defaults and behavior already implied by accepted
requirements. Summarize those defaults together so the user can correct them;
do not turn correctness, consistency, or every internal design choice into an
approval question. For example, a derived forecast normally updates when its
source date is corrected; choosing which source governs that forecast may need
an owner decision.

Work through dependent or consequential questions one at a time. Batch independent,
straightforward questions when useful or requested. Follow the user's preferred
pace. Continue independent investigation and authorized preparation while an
answer is pending; do not implement behavior that depends on an unresolved choice.

Record the agreed outcome, decisions, routine defaults, and remaining blockers in
the existing work record when one is used. Finish planning with the next concrete
action and a concise continuation prompt when handing off; reference the record
instead of repeating instructions. Planning alone does not authorize implementation
or publication, but do not ask again for authorization already granted in the task.
